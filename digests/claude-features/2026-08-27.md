## 4. OpenAI and Broadcom unveil "Jalapeño," OpenAI's first custom inference chip [Other AI]
High confidence — confirmed directly on OpenAI's own site (two posts, Aug 25) plus TechCrunch/VentureBeat/Tom's Hardware/SemiAnalysis coverage. Jalapeño is a purpose-built inference ASIC (not a repurposed training chip), co-developed with Broadcom in a nine-month tape-out cycle partly accelerated by OpenAI's own models, and benchmarked (via SemiAnalysis' InferenceX) beating current state-of-the-art inference hardware on tokens-per-user and throughput-per-watt. Deployment begins late 2026. Pure industry awareness — a first move by a frontier lab into owning its own inference silicon, not just training silicon.

## 5. OpenAI rolls out Zero Data Retention + "Private Safety Processing" for frontier models [Other AI]
High confidence — confirmed on OpenAI's own site (Aug 21) plus independent security-press coverage. Eligible API customers on frontier models get a real zero-retention guarantee (no prompts/outputs kept post-request, no employee content review, no training use without opt-in); a new "Private Safety Processing" layer lets automated abuse-detection run without exposing raw customer content to OpenAI staff, surfacing only a risk-category signal on a hit. Aimed squarely at regulated-data enterprise adoption (finance, health, confidential business data); early customers now, broader rollout in September. No direct Brian-project tie-in, but a notable data-handling precedent worth knowing if data-retention terms ever become a factor in picking a provider for a client-facing build (e.g. CWI AI).
